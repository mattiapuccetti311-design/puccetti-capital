01Investment thesis

We initiate on Hermès with a REDUCE rating and a target price of €871, 31% below the 29 October close of €1,266. Hermès remains the most profitable company in luxury, with a 41% operating margin and €10bn of net cash. The issue is price, not quality: at 27x 2026 earnings the shares still require a discount rate of about 8.3%, well below the 10.6% cost of equity we derive from French government yields.

Three reasons

  • Growth has slowed to mid-single digits. Q3 revenue grew 4.3% at constant currency, the weakest quarter since 2020 and below the 5.2% expected. Ready-to-wear fell 2% and the Middle East fell 4.5%.
  • Currency is eating the growth. Nine-month revenue rose 6.0% at constant rates but only 2.9% in euros. With the dollar weak, reported growth and margins are under pressure in 2026.
  • The premium to peers is still wide. Hermès trades at 17x EBITDA against a peer median of 9.8x. Even the best peer, Richemont, is at 16x with jewellery growing above 20%.
What would make us wrong

Hermès has traded at a premium for twenty years and has rarely disappointed for long. A return to double-digit leather-goods growth in 2027, helped by new workshops and price increases, or a fall in European interest rates, would lift our value quickly: at an 8.6% discount rate our DCF gives €1,209.

02The surprise: even Hermès can disappoint

On 22 October Hermès reported Q3 revenue of €4.10bn, up 4.3% at constant currency against market expectations of about 5.2%. Leather goods still grew 10.8%, but ready-to-wear and accessories fell 2%, and the Middle East fell 4.5% with tourism hit by the regional conflict. The shares fell more than 7% on the day and another 4% the next day, when Kering dropped 17% and Richemont 6%.

The fall is part of a sector re-rating. According to the Financial Times, Europe's six largest luxury groups have lost €251bn of market value in 2026, and LVMH and Hermès account for nearly 90% of it. Hermès shares are down about 44% over twelve months, from a 52-week high of €2,300.

03Ten-year history and estimates

Hermès is a rare case of growth without acquisitions. Revenue tripled from €5.2bn in 2016 to €16.0bn in 2025 (13.3% a year) and the operating margin rose from 32.6% to 41%. The workforce doubled from 12,834 to 26,494, mostly artisans trained in the group's own schools. Growth came from opening leather workshops in France, about one a year (the 24th opened in 2025), and from steady price increases.

Key investments and events

  • 2016: €5.2bn revenue, 32.6% margin, 12,834 employees.
  • 2020: Covid, revenue −7%, the only down year of the decade.
  • 2021-2023: post-Covid boom, revenue +41% then +29%.
  • 2024: capex peaks at €1.07bn for new workshops and stores.
  • 2025: 24th leather workshop opens; €3,000 bonus to every employee.
  • 2026: four more workshops planned to 2030 (Loupes, Charleville-Mézières, Colombelles, Les Andelys).

Hermès has no financial debt and holds about €10bn of net cash. It converts almost all its profit into free cash flow, and pays out only about 42% as ordinary dividends, so cash keeps building. Liquidity is not a risk.

The large cash pile also explains the low return on capital figures some screens show: €10bn earning interest dilutes a business that earns over 40% on its operating assets.

Applying the peer median of 9.8x to Hermès' €7.06bn EBITDA gives €755 per share; the forward P/E median of 19.4x gives €913. Using the upper quartile instead (15.8x EBITDA, 22.6x P/E) gives €1,161 and €1,062. Even with that premium the shares look expensive against peers.

Hermès deserves a premium for its margin and its record, so comparables carry only 40% of our target together.

Recommendation · Reduce · Target €871

Our view is far from consensus: the 23 analysts tracked by StockAnalysis have an average target of about €1,569 and a Buy rating. The difference is mainly the discount rate. Our method uses French government yields near 5%; most brokers use a lower, euro-area rate. We would turn more positive below €950 or if growth returned above 8% at constant currency.

08Risks and catalysts

Upside risks to our view

  • Lower rates. Each point less on the discount rate adds about €130 per share.
  • Leather-goods supply. New workshops lift production by about 6% a year, and demand still exceeds supply.
  • Pricing power. Another price increase is announced for 2027.

Catalysts

  • FY2026 results, 12 February 2027: margin after the currency hit.
  • China and Middle East demand in Q4, the key season.
  • French budget and OAT yields, which drive our cost of capital.
