01Thesis and valuation view

We rate the Series F valuation of Agile Robots as STRETCHED: our probability-weighted fair value is $5.3bn, 37% below the $8.5bn price of the round announced on 12 November 2026. The company is Europe's largest robotics start-up, with more than 20,000 installed systems, a humanoid factory near Munich and revenue that should roughly double this year, partly through acquisitions. But the round values it at about 25 times estimated 2025 revenue and 12 times the 2026 guidance. Listed robot makers trade at 2 to 11 times sales, and Agile's gross margin, about 32% in 2024, is that of a hardware maker. To justify the price at a 22.5% required return and a 4.5x exit multiple, revenue would need to reach about $6.1bn in 2031.

Three points

  • Growth is strong but bought in part. Revenue was about €199m in 2024. Sacra estimates $339m for 2025 and cites guidance of about $690m for 2026. The jump in 2026 includes the former thyssenkrupp Automation Engineering, bought in April 2026 with about 650 staff. Organic growth is not disclosed.
  • The price assumes humanoids work at scale. The Fürstenfeldbruck plant can build up to 10,000 humanoids a year, but the company has published no shipment or order figures. The research humanoid ONE SN starts production at the turn of the year, with 1,000 to 2,000 units expected in 2027.
  • The economics are those of industrial hardware. Gross margin of about 32% and R&D near 42% of sales in 2024 mean losses until 2028 or 2029, the company's own target. Fanuc, ABB and Yaskawa, which are profitable, trade at 1.8 to 4.5 times sales.
What would make us wrong

The round may carry terms that public data do not show. Late-stage investors often receive a liquidation preference, which protects them in a weak exit and makes a headline price higher than the value of an ordinary share. The buyers are also strategic: SoftBank is building a robotics group, Nvidia sells the chips inside the humanoids and Hitachi co-develops factory systems with Agile. If humanoids reach volume sales by 2028, our bull case of $12.9bn would apply. A 10% rise in exit revenue in every scenario only moves our fair value to $5.7bn, so the verdict is not close to the threshold.

02The surprise: a SoftBank round at 8.5 times the 2021 price

On 12 November 2026 Agile Robots announced a $500m Series F at an $8.5bn valuation. SoftBank Group led, as Handelsblatt had first reported. Nvidia, Hitachi, Hitachi Ventures and T.Capital joined. The company says the money will fund its entry into the United States, more production in Germany, new uses for its robots and more engineers. Total funding now stands at about $2bn.

The surprise has two sides. The valuation is high: the last priced round, a $220m Series C in September 2021, valued the company at about $1bn, so the price rose 8.5 times. The amount is low: in June 2026 Bloomberg reported that SoftBank was discussing a round of about $800m. The final round is $300m smaller, which suggests that investors would pay a high price for a smaller stake but did not fill the larger round.

The timing is also unusual for the lead investor. SoftBank reported a net loss of ¥1.12 trillion for July to September 2026, and on 6 November China blocked the Chinese part of its planned purchase of ABB Robotics. A week before the Agile announcement, on 5 November, the US humanoid maker Agility Robotics listed on Nasdaq with sales of only $1.8m and a net loss of $138m. Robotics is in demand from investors, both public and private.

04Business model and revenue

Agile Robots was founded in 2018 by Zhaopeng Chen, the chief executive, and Peter Meusel, as a spin-out of the German Aerospace Center (DLR), which is known for its work on robot arms with a sense of touch. The company sells force-sensitive robot arms (Diana and Thor series), the Franka research arm, a robotic hand, mobile robots for factory logistics (idealworks, with more than 850 units at BMW sites) and its own software. It has added humanoids: the Agile ONE for factories and the smaller ONE SN for research labs, built on Nvidia's GR00T models and Jetson Thor computers.

Much of the growth has come from buying companies. Agile took over the insolvent Franka Emika in 2023 for a reported sum above €30m, and later added idealworks, BÄR Automation, audEERING and, in April 2026, thyssenkrupp Automation Engineering, now Krause Automation. This last deal brought about 650 engineers and ten sites in Europe and North America, and turns Agile into a builder of complete production lines. The main customers are in consumer electronics and cars, with Foxconn and BMW as anchors.

Staff numbers have grown from about 2,500 in November 2025 to more than 3,500 today, over 2,000 of them in research and development. The company holds more than 650 patents.

06Main model: venture capital method

The VC method estimates what the company could be worth at exit in five years and discounts that value at the return a late-stage investor requires. Agile is loss-making, so the range is 20-30%. We use 22.5%, near the low end, because the company already has $600m of revenue, a large installed base and strategic shareholders. We assume 15% dilution from future rounds and employee options, more than for a software company because hardware growth needs capital. The discount factor is 0.85 / 1.225^5 = 0.308.

Our base case takes revenue from $600m in 2026 to $3.0bn in 2031, a compound rate of 38% a year. That would make Agile about half the size of Fanuc's revenue today. The 4.5x exit multiple is that of Fanuc, the most profitable listed robot maker. The bull case assumes $6bn of revenue and a 7x multiple, the level of Teradyne and Symbotic. At the round price, the base case gives an investor only about 6% a year. If the 2026 guidance of $690m is met and the base path is scaled up in proportion, 2031 revenue becomes $3.45bn and the base value $4.78bn, still well below the round.

The weighted result is $5.33bn, which we round to $5.3bn. The two methods agree closely, within 10%, which gives us more confidence than usual in the central value. The round price lies above the whole comparables range and is reached only in the bull case of the VC method. The investors are paying for humanoid success before there is public evidence of humanoid sales.

Verdict · Stretched · Fair value $5.3bn

Our fair value is 37% below the Series F price. Agile Robots is a credible European champion with a strong position in force-sensitive arms and, after Krause, in complete production lines. But its margins are those of a machine builder, and the price assumes software-like multiples. We would move towards Full if the company published audited accounts showing organic growth above 40%, a gross margin above 40% and firm humanoid orders in the thousands.

08Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • Data quality: no audited accounts; revenue figures come from a third party.
  • Integration: five acquisitions since 2023, including a large engineering business with lower margins.
  • Competition: Fanuc, ABB (soon under SoftBank), Chinese cobot makers and humanoid firms such as Figure, Apptronik and NEURA Robotics.
  • Safety rules: the EU Machinery Regulation applies from 20 January 2027 and a humanoid safety standard (ISO 25785-1) is still being written.
  • ESG: our ESG score is 59/100 (Average), held back by the absence of any sustainability report and by limited disclosure on ownership, after claims in 2023 that Chinese investors controlled the company, which it denies.

Catalysts

  • ONE SN production start and the first humanoid delivery figures in 2027.
  • US entry, funded by the Series F.
  • Hitachi partnership on physical AI for factories, signed in September 2026.
  • IPO: the chief executive has mentioned a later stock market listing, which would bring audited accounts.
