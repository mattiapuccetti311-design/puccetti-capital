01Thesis and valuation view

We rate Legora's latest valuation as STRETCHED: our probability-weighted fair value is $7.2bn, 18% below the $8.8bn post-money of the October 2026 Series E. Legora is a real business with real customers. It has passed $200m of annual recurring revenue three years after its founding, and more than half of the 50 largest US law firms use it. But the round values it at 44 times that revenue. To earn a 25% return for a late-stage investor, revenue must reach about $4bn by 2031, twenty times today's level.

Three points

  • Growth is among the fastest ever recorded in enterprise software. Annual recurring revenue (ARR) went from about $1m in March 2024 to $100m in April 2026 and above $200m by October 2026, according to the company. Bessemer, an investor, calls it the fastest enterprise business to reach $100m.
  • Legora is the number two in a two-horse race. Its US rival Harvey reported more than $400m of ARR in September 2026 and is valued at $15.5bn. Both firms raise very large rounds to win the same large law firms.
  • The model makers are moving into legal work. Anthropic launched Claude for Legal in May 2026 and OpenAI is building a legal offer. Legora buys its core technology from these suppliers and now competes with them for the same budgets.
What would make us wrong

The main risk to our view is that growth continues at the current pace for longer than we assume. Legora doubled its ARR in under six months this year. If ARR reaches $280m by December 2026, our estimate, and the market applied the software median of 35.6x to that figure, the comparables value would be $10.0bn and our fair value $8.3bn, close to the round price. A second risk is strategic value: incumbents such as Thomson Reuters or RELX could pay a premium to buy a leading AI platform rather than build one.

02The surprise: a 57% step-up in six months

On 21 October 2026 Legora announced a $550m Series E at an $8.8bn post-money valuation. The round was led by Atreides Management, a Boston investor new to the company. Large public-market funds joined for the first time: Fidelity, Capital Group, Baron Capital and J.P. Morgan. Existing backers including Accel, Benchmark, Bessemer, General Catalyst and ICONIQ also invested.

Only six months earlier, Legora had closed a $600m Series D at $5.6bn. A 57% step-up in half a year is large even by the standards of the 2026 AI market. Press reports in August had mentioned talks above $10bn, so the final price was below the highest rumours. The arrival of mutual-fund investors matters: these are the buyers who usually appear one or two rounds before a stock market listing.

With the round, the CFO said ARR had passed $200m. In-house legal teams, a small share of clients a year ago, made more than 40% of new customers in the third quarter, and the US is now Legora's largest market.

04Business model and revenue

Legora was founded in Stockholm in 2023 by Max Junestrand (CEO), Sigge Labor and August Erséus, under the name Leya. It went through Y Combinator in early 2024 and took its current name in February 2025. Its first large client was the Swedish law firm Mannheimer Swartling.

The product is a workspace where lawyers review documents, research case law, draft contracts and run multi-step tasks with AI agents. It sits on top of models from outside providers. Legora sells per-seat subscriptions, with an estimated average contract of about $95,000 a year (Sacra). Since June 2026 it has started to charge some agent features by usage. Clients include Linklaters, White & Case, Cleary Gottlieb and Goodwin, plus in-house teams at Salesforce and Blackstone.

The company has grown by acquisition as well: five purchases in 2026 (Walter AI, Qura, Graceview, Cadastral and Wexler), each adding a feature such as legal research or regulatory monitoring. Staff went from about 40 to more than 1,000 in around two years, across 18 offices.

Profitability is the open question. The CEO said in late 2025 that gross margins were "okay" but below normal software levels, because every query pays for model inference. With headcount up 25-fold and five acquisitions in a year, we assume the company is loss-making.

06Main model: venture capital method

The VC method estimates what the company could be worth at exit in five years, then discounts that value at the return a late-stage investor requires. We use 25%, the upper part of the 20-30% range for loss-making companies, because Legora depends on suppliers that are also competitors. We assume a further 15% dilution from future rounds and employee options before exit.

Verdict · Stretched · Fair value $7.2bn

The Series E price is 18% above our fair value. It is not far off, but it is slightly more expensive than Harvey on revenue (44x against 39x of ARR) for a company half its size. But it leaves little room for a slowdown, for margin pressure from model costs, or for model makers selling directly to law firms. We would see the valuation as supported if Legora showed ARR above $400m by mid-2027 with improving gross margins.

08Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • Supplier competition: Anthropic and OpenAI now sell legal products directly.
  • Harvey: twice the ARR and more capital; price competition for large firms.
  • Margins: inference costs keep gross margins below software norms.
  • Integration: five acquisitions and 25-fold headcount growth in two years.
  • ESG: our ESG score is 64/100 (Average), held back by the absence of any sustainability reporting so far.

Catalysts

  • Next ARR update, likely with year-end 2026 figures.
  • Move to usage-based pricing, which could raise revenue per client.
  • In-house legal teams, now over 40% of new customers.
  • IPO preparation: mutual funds on the cap table point in that direction.
