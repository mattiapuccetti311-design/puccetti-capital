01Investment thesis

We initiate on adidas with a BUY rating and a target price of €230, 30% above the 11 November close of €177.10. On the evening of 20 October adidas reported a record third-quarter operating profit of €1,008m, against a consensus of €742m, and raised its 2026 profit outlook to about €2.5bn from about €2.3bn. About €200m of the beat came from a refund of US tariffs, so the market first reacted calmly. The shares then rose 5.8% on 29 October, when the full report showed the quality of the quarter, and have gained 22% since the end of September.

Three reasons

  • The underlying beat is real. Without the refund, the Q3 operating profit was about €808m, still 9% above the consensus (J.P. Morgan). Direct-to-consumer sales grew 17%, the gross margin excluding the refund was about 51.6%, and inventories fell 4% from the second quarter.
  • The margin has room to recover. The 2026 operating margin is about 9.3% including the refund, against 11.3% in 2019. Management targets more than 10% in 2028 and mid-teens profit growth a year from 2025 to 2028. Our base case reaches 11.0% in 2030 and stays there.
  • The price assumes little of this. At €177.10 the shares trade at 15.8x our 2027E earnings and 9.7x 2026E EBITDA, below the peer medians of 19.4x and 14.0x. The price implies a discount rate of 10.4%, against our WACC of 8.9%. Free cash flow over the last twelve months was €2.6bn, a yield of 8.6%.
What would make us wrong

Lifestyle footwear, the business built on the Samba and other classics, is flat and the market for it is promotional. Europe, the largest region, fell 4% in Q3. If the classics cycle ends and Nike recovers, growth could fall to mid-single digits and the margin could stall near 9%: that is our bear case, worth €154, or 13% below the price. US tariffs and a strong euro are a running cost of about €400m this year, and the €200m refund will not repeat.

02The surprise: a record quarter, with a refund inside

adidas published preliminary figures after the market close on 20 October, nine days before its full report. Sales rose 9% to a record €7,252m, or 7% in local currencies. The operating profit rose 37% to €1,008m, a margin of 13.9%, against a consensus of €742m compiled by the company. The gross margin rose 2.5 points to 54.3%. Net income from continuing operations rose 48% to €711m and earnings per share reached €4.06, against €2.57 a year earlier.

The market was careful at first. The shares closed 0.1% higher on 20 October and 1.7% higher on 21 October, because analysts such as Metzler pointed out that the higher outlook mostly reflected the one-off refund of about €200m. The full report on 29 October changed the tone: J.P. Morgan noted that the profit beat was still about 9% without the refund, DTC grew 17% and inventories were cut. The shares rose 5.8% that day and another 4.9% on 4 November. Of the €200m higher outlook, €100m is the refund net of a €100m reinvestment in Q4, and €100m comes from the business itself.

Growth came from apparel (+19% in local currencies) and from the company's own channels: e-commerce rose 18% and own retail 16%. Footwear was flat because adidas kept wholesale orders low to avoid discounts in lifestyle footwear, where retailers hold too much stock. Wholesale rose only 2%.

Every region except Europe grew by double digits. Europe fell 4%, partly because a planned SAP migration delayed shipments at the end of September; the company expects high single-digit growth in Europe in Q4. The 30 June inventory figure in the table is derived from the 4% fall the company reports.

03History and estimates

adidas reached an operating margin of 11.3% in 2019, then lost most of it in four years: the pandemic in 2020, the loss of share in China, and the end of the Yeezy partnership in October 2022. Reebok was sold to Authentic Brands in 2022, which is why the figures from 2021 exclude it. In 2023, under the new chief executive Bjørn Gulden, the margin fell to 1.3% and the group made a small loss. Since then sales have grown at a high single-digit rate in local currencies and the margin has rebuilt to 8.3% in 2025, without any Yeezy sales. The workforce is 64,394.

Key events and investments

  • 2021-2022: Reebok sold to Authentic Brands; €2.5bn of buybacks in 2022, just before profits fell.
  • October 2022: the Yeezy partnership ends; the remaining stock is sold over 2023-2024 (about €650m of sales in 2024).
  • January 2023: Bjørn Gulden, previously at Puma, becomes chief executive.
  • March 2026: targets of high single-digit growth a year to 2028 and a margin above 10% in 2028; €1bn buyback launched.
  • June-July 2026: FIFA World Cup with an all-adidas final; marketing peaks in Q2-Q3.
  • Investments: capex of €363-504m a year since 2021 (€477m in 2025 on the company's definition), half in own stores and shop-in-shops, the rest in IT and logistics.

Our 2026E and 2027E revenues are in line with the consensus (€27.13bn and €29.17bn). Our 2027E EPS of €11.18 is 5% below the consensus of €11.73, because we assume no repeat of the refund and a slower Europe. Our view differs from the market on the later years, not on next year.

Cash flow swings with inventory. In 2025 operating cash flow fell to €751m because inventories rose 17% to €5.8bn ahead of the World Cup. In the twelve months to September the stock was sold and free cash flow returned to €2.6bn, enough to pay €491m of dividends and buy back €1bn of shares. The buyback cancelled 6.0m shares and reduced the count to 172.4m.

Adjusted net borrowings were €4,646m at 30 September, or 1.3x EBITDA. This figure includes €2,905m of lease liabilities. Without leases, net financial debt is about €1.74bn.

Equity was €6.66bn at 30 September, including €405m of minorities. In the DCF we treat leases as operating costs (they are already inside the operating profit), so we deduct only the €1.74bn of net financial debt and the €405m of minorities. For the EV/EBITDA comparison, where EBITDA is before lease costs, we deduct the full €4.65bn. We use the 30 September balance sheet and give no credit for the Q4 cash inflow, which is normally strong.

adidas trades at 9.8x EBITDA, 30% below the median of six peers, and at 15.9x forward earnings against 19.4x. Asics, Amer Sports and On grow faster and earn higher margins, so a full peer multiple is too much for adidas. Nike, the closest peer by size and margin, trades at 12.3x.

At the median, last-twelve-month EBITDA of €3.59bn gives €261 per share, and our 2027E EPS of €11.18 at 19.4x gives €216. At the lower quartile the values fall to €162 and €155, close to the price.

The model runs nine full years, 2027 to 2035, from a 2026E base of €27.0bn of sales and €2.5bn of operating profit, with mid-year discounting from 11 November 2026. In the base case sales grow 8% in 2027 and 7.5% in 2028, in line with the company's high single-digit target, then slow to 3% in 2035. The operating margin rises from 9.6% in 2027 to 11.0% in 2030 and stays there, slightly below the 2019 level. D&A and capex exclude leases, which stay inside the operating profit. Enterprise value is €40.8bn, 63% from the terminal value. After €1.74bn of net financial debt and €0.41bn of minorities the equity is worth €38.6bn, or €224 per share. The implied exit multiple is 8.5x EBITDA including leases, below today's 9.7x. Weighting the scenarios 25/50/25 gives €223.

The bear case keeps growth near 5% and the margin at 9.0%, where it is today without the refund: it is worth €154. The bull case has a margin of 12.5% from 2031, close to Asics and On today, and is worth €288. Each point of margin in the long run is worth about €20 per share, which makes the margin, not the discount rate, the main driver of the value. The price of €177.10 implies a discount rate of 10.4% in the base case.

The weighted value is €229.0, which we round to €230. The dividend model is a memo: with a payout of 36% of our 2027E EPS, dividend growth of 12% for five years and then 3%, and a cost of equity of 9.2%, it gives €95. It misses because adidas returns as much cash through buybacks (up to €1bn a year to 2028) as through dividends, so we give it no weight. All three main methods give more than 20% of upside at their central value. Only the lower quartile of peers, the bear case and the low end of analyst targets sit below the price.

Recommendation · Buy · Target €230

Our target is 16% above the consensus average of €198.47 (32 analysts at StockAnalysis, 15 Strong Buy, 9 Buy, 7 Hold, 1 Strong Sell) and inside the range of €156 to €245. Bernstein has €245; UBS, the most cautious, cut to €156 on 3 November. Our near-term earnings are in fact slightly below the consensus. The difference is the long run: we think a margin of 11% is reachable once tariffs and lifestyle footwear normalise, because adidas earned 11.3% in 2019 and now sells far more through its own channels. We would turn neutral if the Q4 report on 3 March 2027 shows Europe still falling, or if 2027 guidance puts the operating margin below 9.5%.

08Risks and catalysts

Downside risks to our view

  • Lifestyle footwear. The terrace and low-profile classics carried growth in 2024-2025. The market is now promotional and adidas footwear is flat. A longer downturn would cut both growth and gross margin.
  • Tariffs and currency. Higher US tariffs and a strong euro cost about €400m in 2026. The €200m refund is a one-off.
  • Competition. Nike is in a reset and could regain share; On, Asics and Hoka continue to take share in running.
  • Europe. Sales fell 4% in Q3. The company blames timing and cautious sell-in; a second weak quarter would point to demand.
  • Governance. In July 2025 adidas opened an internal investigation after a whistleblower alleged kickbacks in its China business. We found no outcome.
  • Margin assumption. Each point of long-run margin is worth about €20 per share in our base case.
  • ESG. Our ESG score is 85/100 (Leader): MSCI AAA, CDP Climate A and a low Sustainalytics risk, less 5 points for a 2025 court ruling against a climate claim in its advertising.

Catalysts

  • Christmas trading and Q4 Europe, where the company expects high single-digit growth after the ERP delay.
  • Full-year 2026 results and 2027 guidance: listed for 3 March 2027 by MarketScreener, not confirmed by the company.
  • Share buybacks: the board may buy back up to €1bn in each of 2027 and 2028, subject to cash flow.
  • Annual general meeting in May 2027: dividend for 2026, which we estimate at €3.50.
  • Q1 2027 results, listed for 26 April 2027 by MarketScreener.
