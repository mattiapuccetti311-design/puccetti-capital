01Investment thesis

We initiate on BNP Paribas with a BUY rating and a target price of €116, 33% above the 29 October close of €87.65. At 0.89x tangible book value, the share price implies a long-term return on tangible equity of about 10%. The bank is already earning 13.2% this year and targets more than 13% in 2028. We think the gap is a discount for French political risk that BNP's business mix does not justify.

Three reasons

  • Profitability has stepped up. Nine-month net income rose 19% to €11.0bn and ROTE reached 13.2%, against 11.4% a year earlier. Revenue grew 10% while costs grew 7%, so the cost/income ratio is falling as planned.
  • France is a smaller part of the bank than the price suggests. France generated under 10% of 2025 pre-tax income, half its 2016 share. The €19bn of French government bonds is mostly at amortised cost: another 50 bp of spread would cost 0.02 points of CET1.
  • Shareholders are paid to wait. A 60% payout gives a 7% dividend yield on 2026 estimates, plus a €1.3bn buyback starting in November. Management is considering raising the payout to 70%.
What would make us wrong

The market may be right that French risk is not finished. A sovereign downgrade, a budget crisis or a domestic recession would raise BNP's cost of funding and of risk at the same time. The other open item is the US Sudan litigation, where the bank has made no provision.

02The surprise: a beat the market ignored

BNP Paribas is a case of a stock that surprised the market twice in one year. It rose about 75% from €65 in November 2025 to a record €113.8 in mid-August 2026, its first new high since 2008. It then fell 27% in two months as French 10-year yields climbed from 3.8% to above 5% and the budget crisis turned into street protests. On 28 October the bank reported Q3 net income of €3.46bn, 7% above the €3.23bn consensus. The shares opened 1.5% lower and finished the day flat.

BNP's capital position has improved steadily, from 12.6% CET1 at end-2025 to 12.9% in September despite a 15 bp hit from buying Athlon. That leaves room for the €1.3bn buyback and possibly a higher payout from 2027.

The French sovereign exposure is small relative to a balance sheet above €3,000bn, and most of it is held to maturity, so price swings do not reach capital. The bigger French risk is indirect: a weaker economy would raise loan losses in French retail banking, and higher OAT yields lift the cost of funding for all French issuers.

05Valuation against European peers

Banks are valued on book value and earnings, not on cash flow: a bank's debt is its raw material, so a DCF on free cash flow has no meaning. The key relationship is between price-to-tangible-book and return on equity: banks that earn more than their cost of equity trade above book.

The fit is strong: return on equity explains about 80% of the difference in valuation. On its trailing ROE of 10.5%, BNP should trade at 1.04x tangible book, or €102. On our 2026 ROTE of 12.5% the same line gives €129. We retain the trailing figure to stay consistent with the peers' trailing data.

On P/E the picture is clearer still. Non-French peers trade at 9.6-10.5x forward earnings, French banks at 6.4-7.1x. Applying the peer median of 9.6x to our 2026 EPS of €12.30 gives €118.

The excess-return model gets the largest weight because it ties value directly to the profitability the bank reports and targets. The market-based methods get 40% together. We show the French-peers-only P/E as a memo line: it is the one way to justify today's price, but only by assuming BNP deserves the same discount as banks with three to four times its share of revenue in France.

Recommendation · Buy · Target €116

Our target is 33% above the current price and close to the 19-analyst consensus of €119. Every method except the French-only P/E points above today's price. We would revisit the rating if Moody's cut France's Aa3 rating, bringing it in line with the A+ already given by S&P and Fitch, if the cost of risk rose above 50 bp, or if the Sudan appeal failed and a provision became necessary.

09Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • French sovereign stress. The 10-year OAT went above 5% in October and Moody's has a negative outlook on France's Aa3 rating.
  • Sudan litigation (US). A jury awarded $20.75m to three plaintiffs in October 2025; plaintiffs' lawyers speak of 20,000 potential claimants. BNP is appealing and has made no provision.
  • Trading revenue. Q3 equity trading grew 50%; that level is unlikely to repeat every quarter.
  • Arval. Weak used-car prices cut leasing revenue by 29% in Q3.
  • Cost of equity. Each 50 bp on the cost of equity moves our main value by about €9.

Catalysts

  • €1.3bn buyback from 2 November 2026.
  • Strategic plan, 2 February 2027: 2030 targets and a possible payout increase to 70%.
  • CET1 above 13% at year-end, with the BMCI sale.
  • French 2027 budget vote and the next rating reviews.
  • Sudan appeal ruling at the US Court of Appeals.
