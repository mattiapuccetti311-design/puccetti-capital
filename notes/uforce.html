01Thesis and valuation view

We rate the Series A valuation of UFORCE as STRETCHED: our probability-weighted fair value is $4.2bn, 30% below the $6.0bn post-money price of the round announced on 12 November 2026. UFORCE is a London holding company that groups nine Ukrainian makers of uncrewed systems, including the MAGURA sea drones credited with sinking or disabling more than a dozen Russian warships. The company says revenue doubled in the past year and staff tripled to more than 1,000. On our estimate of about $500m of 2026 revenue, the round is worth 12 times sales. Listed defence groups trade at 2.3 to 3.5 times sales. To justify the price at a 20% required return and a 4x exit multiple, revenue would need to reach about $4.15bn in 2031, a growth rate of 53% a year for five years and above our bull case.

Three points

  • The products are proven in war. MAGURA boats have been in Black Sea service since 2023. The company says its NEMESIS strike drones have flown more than 300,000 combat sorties with over 100 Ukrainian units. Few defence start-ups have this kind of field record.
  • The numbers are not. UFORCE has published no revenue, profit or cash-flow figure. Reports variously describe 450% growth in 2025 as revenue growth or bookings growth, and the chief executive told CNBC only that bookings more than doubled from a base of several hundred million dollars. A Big Four auditor has been hired but not named.
  • The main customer is a country at war. The chief executive says Ukraine's needs come first. A ceasefire would cut the most urgent demand, while export sales to NATO countries are still at the stage of exercises, a memorandum with ReconCraft in the United States and a UK production pledge without named sites.
What would make us wrong

The investors include two state funds, the UK National Wealth Fund and the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund, and Valor Equity Partners, an early backer of Anduril. They may have seen audited figures that we have not. Private defence-technology rounds are also priced well above listed peers: Anduril raised at 14 times its 2026 revenue projection in May and Helsing at $18bn in July. If UFORCE converts its combat record into multi-year NATO orders, our bull case of $9.6bn would apply. A 10% rise in revenue in every scenario only moves our fair value to $4.4bn, so the verdict is not close to the threshold.

02The surprise: a Series A six times the March price

On 12 November 2026 UFORCE announced a $700m Series A at a $6bn valuation. Valor Equity Partners and the UK National Wealth Fund co-led. The wealth fund invested £113m, about $150m, its largest defence investment so far. The US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund, Menlo Ventures, Xantium and Hiro Capital joined, and existing investors Shield Capital, Ballistic Ventures, Lakestar and Squadra raised their stakes. Retired General Christopher Cavoli, NATO's former Supreme Allied Commander Europe and now a Valor partner, joined the board.

The surprise is the speed. In March 2026 the company came out of stealth with a $50m round at just over $1bn. In August Sifted reported talks above $4bn, and in September Bloomberg reported about $500m at about $5bn. The final round is 40% larger in dollars and 20% higher in price than the September report, and the chief executive says UFORCE turned down offers at higher valuations. The round is called a Series A, but at $700m it ranks among the largest first institutional rounds in European defence.

The money is meant for production in Ukraine and allied countries, research, new products and possible acquisitions. In the United Kingdom, the wealth fund expects British production of the MAGURA MV7 and MV11 boats and NEMESIS systems, with more than 70% British content in each UK-built boat. No site, start date or job count has been published, and UFORCE UK Ltd was incorporated only on 20 October 2026.

04Business model and revenue

UFORCE sells uncrewed systems in four families: MAGURA sea drones (from the 5.5-metre V5, with a 200 kg payload and a unit cost of about $250,000 to $300,000, to the 12-metre MV11 mothership unveiled in August 2026), NEMESIS aerial strike drones, LIUT ground robots and PREDATOR remote weapon turrets. A command-and-control software layer links drones from several makers on one map; the company says it has been used in more than 150,000 combat missions.

The chief executive is Oleg Rogynskyy, who founded the US sales software company People.ai. The board is chaired by Oleksiy Honcharuk, Prime Minister of Ukraine in 2019 and 2020, and includes Sir Ben Wallace, a former UK defence secretary. The group has more than 1,000 staff at over 15 sites in six European countries, and hubs in London, Kyiv and Washington.

The business case is low cost. A MAGURA V5 costs a small fraction of the warships it has attacked, and the chief executive's stated aim is to cut the cost of defence to about one hundredth of the cost of attack. Export demand is building: MAGURA boats were used by US troops at the Balikatan exercise in the Philippines in April and in a US Army exercise in Michigan this autumn.

Dealroom reported in March that the group is profitable; margins are not public, so we value the company on sales.

UFORCE grows much faster than the listed groups, so we apply the upper quartile, 3.40x, to our 2027 revenue estimate of $750m. That gives $2.55bn in a year, or $2.13bn today after discounting at 20%. Listed peers grow at 14% to 33% a year, against about 100% for UFORCE in 2026, so this value is likely too low on its own. We therefore blend it 50/50 with the private benchmark: Anduril's $61bn round in May on its $4.3bn 2026 projection, 14.2x, applied to our 2026E revenue, gives $7.09bn. The blended central value is $4.61bn. The low case is the listed median, 2.95x, on 2026E revenue: $1.48bn. The high case is the Anduril multiple, $7.09bn. We give this method 40% of our fair value.

06Main model: venture capital method

The VC method estimates what the company could be worth at exit in five years and discounts that value at the return a late-stage investor requires. The range is about 15% for a profitable company and 20-30% for a loss-making one. UFORCE is reported to be profitable, but the figure is not audited and most sales go to one customer at war, so we use 20%. We assume 10% dilution from employee options and share-funded acquisitions, which the company says it is considering. The discount factor is 0.90 / 1.20^5 = 0.362.

Our base case takes revenue from $500m in 2026 to $1.86bn in 2031, a compound rate of 30% a year. That is about the size of Kratos or AeroVironment today. The 4.0x exit multiple sits above the listed upper quartile, 3.40x, because UFORCE would still be growing faster. The bear case assumes 10% growth after a ceasefire and the multiple of a small listed contractor. The bull case assumes 50% growth and 7.0x, half of Anduril's private multiple. At the round price, the base case gives an investor only about 2% a year. The company has no public revenue target, so we cannot show a management case; the revenue needed to justify $6bn at 20% and 4.0x is about $4.15bn in 2031.

The weighted result is $4.18bn, which we round to $4.2bn. The two methods are within 20% of each other. The round price lies inside both ranges but above both central values: it is reached in the VC method only in the bull case and in the comparables only with a multiple close to Anduril's. Investors are paying for a NATO-scale export business before there are public NATO orders.

Verdict · Stretched · Fair value $4.2bn

Our fair value is 30% below the Series A price. UFORCE has products that work in combat, strong boards and state backing on both sides of the Atlantic. But it publishes no accounts, its ownership is not fully disclosed and its demand depends on the course of one war. We would move towards Full if the company published audited 2026 accounts showing revenue of at least $600m with a solid margin, and signed multi-year production contracts with the Royal Navy, the US forces or another NATO buyer.

08Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • Data quality: no audited accounts; revenue, bookings and combat figures are self-reported and sometimes conflict.
  • Ceasefire: a peace deal would reduce urgent Ukrainian demand, the core of current sales.
  • Export rules: Ukrainian rules on producing abroad and the ownership of designs between the London parent and Ukrainian subsidiaries are not public.
  • Competition: Helsing, Quantum Systems, Stark, Tekever and Anduril, all well funded, and Ukrainian rivals such as Fire Point.
  • ESG: our ESG score is 46/100 (Weak), held back by the absence of any published accounts or sustainability data and by owners who are not named in the UK register.

Catalysts

  • First UK production site and the first Royal Navy or US order for MAGURA hulls.
  • ReconCraft production in the US, under the memorandum signed in July 2026.
  • Audited accounts from the Big Four auditor the company says it has hired.
  • Acquisitions, which the chief executive lists among next year's plans.
