01Investment thesis

We initiate on Randstad with a BUY rating and a target price of €51, 22.7% above the 18 November close of €41.57. On 21 October the world's largest staffing group reported third-quarter organic growth of 3.9%, against about 2.9% expected, and underlying EBITA of €211m, against about €200m. The shares rose 20.2% that day, their best session in 24 years. We think the market still prices Randstad as if margins stay near the bottom of the cycle for a decade.

Three reasons

  • Profit now grows faster than sales. Gross profit rose only 1% organically, but operating costs fell and underlying EBITA rose 10%. Its recovery ratio, which compares the change in EBITA with the change in gross profit over four quarters, reached 108%. US Operational (blue-collar) work grew 19% and the North American margin rose 70 basis points to 5.3%.
  • The price implies a harsh cycle. At €41.57 the shares trade at 13.8x our 2027E earnings and an 8% free cash flow yield on 2025. Our base DCF gives €57 per share with a mid-cycle margin of 3.9%, still below the 4.2-4.7% reached in 2019 and 2021-2023. The current price implies a WACC of about 9.6%, against our 7.8%.
  • The balance sheet and the dividend floor limit the downside. Net debt excluding leases is €1.15bn, or 1.5x EBITDA. The regular dividend has a floor of €1.62 per share, a 3.9% yield, and the sale of the European and Australian technology consulting unit to LTM will bring about €160m.
What would make us wrong

The sell side is cautious: 6 of 18 analysts rate the shares a Sell and the average target is €38.08. Their main fear is structural. AI tools could shrink demand for white-collar temps and permanent placement, and the gross margin keeps falling year on year (−80, −70 and −60 basis points in the first three quarters of 2026). If the margin stays at 3.2% and growth slows to 1.5-2%, our bear case gives €38, below today's price.

02The surprise: one point of extra growth, ten points of extra profit

Randstad had already beaten growth forecasts in July, when the shares rose about 7%. The third quarter went further. Organic revenue per working day grew 3.9%, against an analyst average of 2.9%, and growth improved every month. September was the strongest month and the company said volumes rose again in early October, helped by logistics clients preparing for the holiday season.

The shares closed at €41.25 on 21 October, up 20.2% from €34.33. They have held the gain and closed at €41.57 on 18 November. Some brokers did not follow: Kepler Cheuvreux cut the shares to Sell (€37) on 26 October and Exane BNP Paribas did the same (€35) on 3 November, both arguing that the gross margin is still falling.

03History and estimates

Staffing is one of the most cyclical sectors in Europe. Revenue fell 12.5% in 2020, then rose 19% in 2021 and 12% in 2022 to a record €27.6bn. From 2023 European industry and US white-collar hiring slowed at the same time, and revenue fell three years in a row to €23.1bn in 2025. The underlying EBITA margin fell from 4.7% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2024 and 2025. 2026 is the first year of profit growth since 2022.

Key investments and events

  • 2016: buys Monster Worldwide, the US job board, for about $429m.
  • 2021-2023: returns cash after record profits, including special dividends and a €400m buyback launched in 2023.
  • 2024: revenue falls 5%; net income drops to €123m after a €121m goodwill impairment and €139m of fair value losses on loans and commitments.
  • 2025: "Partner for Talent" strategy built on four specialisations: Operational, Professional, Digital and Enterprise. SBTi validates the climate targets.
  • 2026: agrees to sell its technology consulting unit in Europe and Australia to LTM for an EV of €160m; LTM becomes its partner for an Indian capability centre.
  • Workforce: average corporate staff fell from 41,720 FTE in Q4 2023 to 37,010 in Q3 2026, while about 560,900 talent worked through Randstad each week in 2025.

Our 2026 estimate adds a fourth quarter of €6.08bn revenue and €215m EBITA to the first nine months, which gives €754m, or 3.2%. Our 2026 EPS of €2.67 is 1% above the consensus of €2.64. For 2027 we assume 4% organic growth and a margin of 3.5%, the level of the third quarter, which gives EPS of €3.01 against a consensus of €2.93.

Randstad needs very little capital: capex is below 0.3% of sales. Cash flow depends on working capital, which rises when growth returns, and on lease payments for branches, about €240-270m a year. "Lease repayments and other" is the gap between StockAnalysis free cash flow and the company's own figure, which deducts lease repayments.

Generous payouts in 2022-2024, at the top of the cycle, took net debt from €0.3bn to €1.3bn. It fell to €1.0bn at the end of 2025 and was €1.15bn in September 2026, after the April dividend. Leverage of 1.5x is comfortable for an asset-light group. We expect new buybacks only once leverage is back near 1x.

Randstad trades exactly at the peer median on EV/EBITDA, above the two generalists Adecco and ManpowerGroup and far below Recruit (which owns Indeed) and Robert Half (whose profits have collapsed). On P/E, it trades between the generalists and the specialists.

Applying the median of 11.1x to Randstad's trailing EBITDA of €779m gives €43 per share; the interquartile range gives €34 to €95. The P/E median of 16.8x on our 2026E EPS gives €45, with a range of €27 to €65. We use the medians, not the upper quartile: Randstad is a generalist with a margin below Recruit's.

The model covers the ten years 2027 to 2036, with mid-year discounting from 18 November 2026; we ignore the last weeks of 2026. Cash flow is EBITA after one-offs and a 30% tax, plus D&A, minus capex, working capital and lease interest. Because lease payments sit inside the cash flow, we deduct net debt excluding leases. In the base case revenue grows 4% a year from 2027 to 2029, from a 2027 base without the LTM unit, then slows to 2% by 2035. The margin rises from 3.5% in 2027 to 3.9% in 2030 and stays there. Enterprise value is €11.1bn, 59% from the terminal value. We deduct €1,147m of net debt and €182m for the preference shares (their €8.2m dividend capitalised at 4.5%), and add €160m from the LTM sale. Equity is €9.9bn, or €57 per share. The implied exit multiple is 10.6x EBITDA, below today's 11.1x. Weighting the scenarios 25/50/25 gives €57.

The margin is the key assumption. Each 0.1 point of EBITA margin from 2030 onwards is worth about €1.5 per share. Our base of 3.9% is below the margins of 2019 and 2021-2023, so it already allows for some lasting damage from AI and lower gross margins. The bear case, with 3.2% for ever, is about where the share trades now.

Recommendation · Buy · Target €51

Our target is 34% above the consensus average of €38.08 from 18 analysts tracked by StockAnalysis, of whom only 3 rate the shares a Buy. The gap comes from the long term: the consensus treats today's 3.1-3.5% margin as close to normal, while we expect 3.9% by 2030. We accept the risk of AI and lower gross margins, and our base is already below the margins of 2019 and 2021-2023. We would move to Hold if the gross margin keeps falling by more than 40 basis points a year in 2027 or if the 2027 EBITA margin stays below 3.3%.

08Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • The cycle. Staffing is the first sector to fall in a recession. In 2020 revenue fell 12.5% and the margin fell to 3.3%.
  • AI and disintermediation. AI tools may cut demand for white-collar temps and for recruiters, and job platforms can bypass agencies. Randstad's own AI tools are a defence but also lower prices.
  • Gross margin. Large clients grow fastest and pay less. Labour rules (the Dutch agreement, French health care price caps) can also squeeze margins.
  • Currency and the US. North America is about 20% of revenue and the region with the highest growth.
  • ESG. Randstad scores 83/100 on our ESG method (Leader), from a negligible Sustainalytics risk of 8.4; MSCI and CDP grades are not public, so the score rests on one provider.

Catalysts

  • Q4 and FY2026 results, expected 17 February 2027 (MarketScreener; not yet confirmed by the company), with the capital return proposal.
  • Closing of the LTM sale, expected at the end of 2026 or early 2027, worth about €160m.
  • Holiday logistics season in the US and Europe, which drove the early October volume gains.
  • Gross margin in Q4, which management expects to be broadly stable on Q3.
  • European PMIs and US payrolls, which lead staffing demand by a few months.
