01Thesis and valuation view

We rate Pigment's latest valuation as STRETCHED: our probability-weighted fair value is $1.5bn, 35% below the $2.3bn post-money of the October 2026 Series E. Pigment is a strong company. It has passed $100m of annual recurring revenue, its customers include L'Oréal, Siemens and Unilever, and it is the leading European name in business planning software. But the round values it at 23 times that revenue, while listed software groups trade at 2 to 7 times. To earn a 22.5% return, revenue must reach about $1.1bn by 2031, eleven times today's level.

Three points

  • Growth is high but slower than the headline. The company says ARR doubled for the second year running. Third-party data show $50m in May 2025 and $100m in October 2026, which is 17 months, or about 60% a year. The CEO expects ARR to double again to near $200m next year.
  • The market for software has been repriced. Workday trades at 4.7 times revenue, ServiceNow at 7.4 and HubSpot at 3.0. Pigment's multiple is more than three times the group's upper quartile. Only Datadog, at 18.6 times, is close.
  • AI cuts both ways. Pigment says 85% of new metrics on its platform are now created by AI agents. That shows the product is current. It also raises the question of whether planning software sold per user keeps its pricing when agents do the work.
What would make us wrong

The main risk to our view is that growth stays above 70% for longer than we assume. If growth stays this high and the market keeps paying 10x revenue at exit, with a 17.5% required return, our base case would be worth $2.7bn, above the round price (see the sensitivity table in section 06). A second risk is scarcity value: Pigment is the flagship of the new Scaleup Europe Fund, and public investors in Europe have few such assets to buy. A strategic buyer such as SAP, Workday or Oracle could pay a premium to own a modern planning engine.

02The surprise: a $2.3bn price in a cheaper software market

On 21 October 2026 Pigment announced a $250m Series E at a $2.3bn post-money valuation. Bloomberg had reported the round a day earlier. The Scaleup Europe Fund, managed by EQT, led it. This fund is backed by the European Commission and private investors, and a fund that began investing this year. World Innovation Lab joined, and existing investors Greenoaks, ICONIQ, IVP and Meritech also took part. Nvidia plans to invest, but its stake is not final and its size is not disclosed.

The last round was a $145m Series D in April 2024, reported by Fortune at a valuation above $1bn. The new price is more than double that. What stands out is the timing. Listed application software now trades at low multiples: Workday is at 4.7 times revenue and HubSpot at 3.0. A private round at 23 times revenue stands far apart from that market. It tells us that large investors still pay a high price for the few software companies growing near 100%.

With the round, the company said its customers now include Anthropic, ElevenLabs, Snowflake and Uber, and that L'Oréal is moving salary planning for more than 95,000 staff onto the platform. Most of the money will go to research and to the Graphite engine and AI agents.

04Business model and revenue

Pigment was founded in Paris in 2019 by Eléonore Crespo, a former Google data analyst, and Romain Niccoli, a co-founder and former technical chief of the advertising group Criteo. Both are co-CEOs. Susan Phan joined as chief financial officer in September 2026, a step that usually comes before a public listing. Crespo has said an IPO is far off.

The product replaces spreadsheets and older planning tools in finance, sales, HR and supply chain. Users build budgets and forecasts on a shared model. Its engine, called Graphite, recalculates large models quickly, and AI agents now build and update parts of the model. The company sells subscriptions to large groups, and a few customers pay for tens of thousands of users, as L'Oréal does. Rivals are Anaplan, Oracle and SAP planning tools, Workday Adaptive Planning, and newer AI-native start-ups.

The company has about 500 employees according to a database estimate; it does not publish the number. We could not find any information on profit or cash burn, and we assume Pigment is still loss-making given the pace of hiring and research.

06Main model: venture capital method

The VC method estimates what the company could be worth at exit in five years, then discounts that value at the return a late-stage investor requires. We use 22.5%, in the middle of the 20-30% range for loss-making companies. Pigment has more revenue and a longer record than a company founded in 2023, but it faces the threat of AI agents to seat-based pricing. We assume a further 15% dilution from future rounds and employee options.

Our base case already assumes revenue grows from about $85m in 2026 to $700m in 2031, a compound rate of 52% a year. For scale, $700m would put Pigment at the size monday.com has today. An exit multiple of 7x is the upper quartile of the listed peers today, which fits a software company still growing 30% a year. The bull case assumes Pigment passes $1bn of revenue and is valued like Datadog is today, at the top of the peer range.

The weighted result is $1.47bn, which we round to $1.5bn. The two methods are 45% apart, and the comparables method is the lower one, so our result rests on the VC method. The gap to the round, 35%, is more than twice our 15% threshold for a Stretched verdict. Only the bull case of the VC method and the high case of the comparables method reach the round price.

Verdict · Stretched · Fair value $1.5bn

The Series E price is 35% above our fair value. Investors in the round are paying for growth that is real but already slowing, at a multiple the listed software market no longer pays. Meeting the CEO's target of $200m of ARR by the end of 2027 would not change our view, because our base case already assumes it. We would see the valuation as supported only if growth stayed above 70% beyond 2027 and the company showed that AI agents lift its revenue per customer instead of cutting it.

08Risks and catalysts

Risks

  • Pricing model: AI agents may cut the number of paid seats.
  • Incumbents: SAP, Oracle and Workday bundle planning with their other products.
  • Growth gap: the "doubling" claim is not borne out by the ARR data.
  • Cost base: about 500 staff and heavy research spending; profit is not disclosed.
  • ESG: our ESG score is 67/100 (Strong), held back by the lack of any published sustainability report or emissions figures.

Catalysts

  • Year-end ARR update, against our $130m estimate and the $200m target for 2027.
  • Nvidia stake, once finalised.
  • Large wins, such as the L'Oréal salary-planning rollout.
  • IPO preparation: a new CFO and public-market investors on the way in.
