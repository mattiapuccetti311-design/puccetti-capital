    How to read the figures

    Returns are total returns: share prices are adjusted for dividends and splits, so a dividend counts as if it were reinvested. The simulation converts euros into the share's currency on the purchase date and back at today's rate, so currency moves are included. Twelve-month scenarios use analysts' lowest, median and highest price targets with today's exchange rate. Targets are opinions, not forecasts we endorse, and they are often wrong.

      ESG ratings

      How sustainable is each company?

      Each company covered receives a Puccetti Capital Research ESG score out of 100. For listed companies it combines the main public ratings; for start-ups, which have no public rating, it is our own assessment on five pillars. The grade is Leader (80 and above), Strong (65-79), Average (50-64), Weak (35-49) or Laggard (below 35).

      TableESG ranking of covered companies
      Score out of 100. Listed: average of MSCI, Sustainalytics and CDP Climate, minus a controversy deduction. Start-ups: Puccetti Capital Research assessment.

      ESG risk of every company in the five markets

      Beyond the companies we cover in our notes, every constituent of the five indices is listed here with its Sustainalytics ESG Risk Rating, as published on Yahoo Finance. The risk score measures the ESG risk a company leaves unmanaged: lower is better. We convert it to our 0-100 scale with the same rule as above (100 − 2 × risk score), then deduct points for controversies of level 3 (−5), 4 (−10) and 5 (−15).

      Source: Sustainalytics ESG Risk Ratings via Yahoo Finance, refreshed monthly. Risk levels: Negligible below 10, Low 10-20, Medium 20-30, High 30-40, Severe 40 and above. Controversy level from 0 (none) to 5 (severe). Many Chinese and Japanese companies have no published score.

      Method

      Listed companies. Three public ratings are converted to a 0-100 scale and averaged. MSCI ESG Rating: AAA = 100, AA = 85, A = 70, BBB = 55, BB = 40, B = 25, CCC = 10. Sustainalytics ESG Risk Rating (lower is better): 100 − 2 × risk score. CDP Climate: A = 100, A- = 90, B = 75, B- = 65, C = 50, C- = 40, D = 25, D- = 15. We then deduct 5 points for a moderate controversy and 10 for a significant one (15 for severe). Other ratings, such as ISS or S&P Global, are shown for information.

      Start-ups. No agency rates them, so we score five pillars out of 20 each from public sources: disclosure, environment, social, governance and controversies (20 means none). This is a judgement, and each score is explained with its source.

      Ratings agencies disagree often, because they measure different things: MSCI and S&P Global look at how a company manages ESG issues, Sustainalytics at the risk left unmanaged, CDP at climate disclosure and action. A single score hides those differences, so the detail is always shown.

      Methodology

      How a note is built

      Listed companies receive a rating based on the potential between the share price and our weighted target price at the date of the note.

      Buyabove +15%
      Accumulate+5% to +15%
      Hold−5% to +5%
      Reducebelow −5%

      Which companies

      Each note covers a company that surprised the market in recent weeks, on results, guidance, a deal or a funding round. Sectors rotate, and about one note in three covers a start-up.

      Listed companies

      Industrial, consumer and technology companies are valued with a ten-year DCF on free cash flow with Bear, Base and Bull scenarios, trading comparables and, where relevant, a dividend model. Banks and insurers are valued with an excess-return model on tangible book value, a regression of price-to-tangible-book on return on equity across peers, P/E comparables and a dividend model.

      Start-ups and private companies

      We compare our fair value with the latest funding round or secondary-market price. The main method is the venture capital method: a value at exit in five years, discounted at the return a late-stage investor requires (20-30% for loss-making companies, about 15% for profitable ones), with Bear, Base and Bull scenarios weighted 25/50/25. Listed comparables serve as a cross-check. The verdict is Supported (more than 15% above the last valuation), Full (within 15%) or Stretched (more than 15% below).

      Cost of capital

      Local 10-year government yield, a 5.5% equity risk premium and a Blume-adjusted five-year beta. Each note shows the effect of these choices in a sensitivity table.

      Facts, estimates, opinion

      Reported figures are marked A and sourced; estimates are marked E; opinion is limited to the thesis, the boxes and the recommendation.
